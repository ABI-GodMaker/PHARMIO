Master Etiology, Pathophysiology, Pharmacotherapy & Patient Counseling for D.Pharm (ER2020) Exams.

What is Hypertension?

Hypertension (HTN) is a chronic medical condition where the systemic arterial blood pressure is persistently elevated. It forces the heart to pump harder than normal to circulate blood through blood vessels.

Known as the "Silent Killer" because it often presents zero initial symptoms while silently damaging target organs like the heart, kidneys, brain, and eyes.

JNC-8 & ISH BP Classification

Etiology & Pathophysiology (RAAS Mechanism)

Types & Causes

  • Primary (Essential) HTN (~90-95%): Unknown exact cause. Linked to genetics, obesity, excess salt intake, age, stress, and lack of exercise.
  • Secondary HTN (5-10%): Secondary to underlying conditions like Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD), Renal Artery Stenosis, Cushing's syndrome, or drugs (NSAIDs, Oral Contraceptives).

The RAAS Axis Breakdown

1. Kidney Secretion: Decreased renal perfusion triggers Renin release from Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells.
2. Angiotensinogen Conversion: Renin converts Angiotensinogen (liver) to Angiotensin I.
3. ACE Action: ACE (Lungs) converts Angiotensin I into Angiotensin II (Potent Vasoconstrictor).
4. Aldosterone & BP Rise: Angiotensin II triggers Aldosterone release → Sodium & Water Retention → High Blood Pressure!

Pharmacological Management (Anti-Hypertensives)

Select a drug class below to study Mechanism of Action (MOA), key drugs, side effects, and D.Pharm exam notes:

1. ACE Inhibitors ("-pril" suffix)

Examples: Enalapril, Captopril, Ramipril, Lisinopril

Mechanism of Action (MOA): Inhibits Angiotensin Converting Enzyme (ACE), blocking conversion of Angiotensin I to Angiotensin II. Decreases vasoconstriction & aldosterone release.

Adverse Effects: Dry Cough (due to Bradykinin accumulation), Hyperkalemia, Angioedema, Hypotension.

Exam Pearl: Contraindicated in Pregnancy (Teratogenic)! Dry cough is a classic board exam question.

2. ARBs - Angiotensin Receptor Blockers ("-sartan")

Examples: Losartan, Telmisartan, Valsartan

MOA: Selectively blocks AT1 receptors. Prevents Angiotensin II binding directly without affecting Bradykinin levels.

Adverse Effects: Hyperkalemia, Dizziness, Renal function changes (No dry cough!).

1. Thiazide Diuretics

Examples: Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ), Chlorthalidone, Indapamide

MOA: Inhibits Na+ / Cl- cotransporter in early Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT) → increases water and salt excretion.

Adverse Effects: Hypokalemia (Low K+), Hyperuricemia (Gout risk), Hyperglycemia.

2. Loop Diuretics & Potassium-Sparing

Loop: Furosemide (Lasix), Torsemide (Acts on Thick Ascending Loop of Henle — high potency).

K+ Sparing: Spironolactone (Aldosterone antagonist), Amiloride.

Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs)

Dihydropyridines (Vascular selective): Amlodipine, Nifedipine → Cause smooth muscle relaxation/vasodilation.

Non-Dihydropyridines (Cardiac selective): Verapamil, Diltiazem → Reduce heart rate and myocardial contractility.

Adverse Effects: Ankle Edema (peripheral swelling with Amlodipine), Headache, Flushing, Constipation (Verapamil).

Beta-Adrenergic Blockers ("-olol")

Examples: Atenolol, Metoprolol (Beta-1 Selective), Propranolol (Non-selective).

MOA: Blocks β1 receptors on myocardium → decreases heart rate (HR) and cardiac output (CO). Also decreases renin release.

Adverse Effects: Bradycardia, Fatigue, Bronchospasm (Avoid non-selective β-blockers in Asthma!).

Warning: Never stop Beta-blockers abruptly due to rebound hypertension risk!

Non-Pharmacological (Lifestyle)

  • Salt Restriction: Limit sodium intake to < 2.0g/day (< 5g NaCl).
  • DASH Diet: Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (Rich in fruits, vegetables, potassium, low saturated fat).
  • Weight Management: Maintain BMI between 18.5 – 24.9 kg/m².
  • Exercise: At least 150 mins/week of moderate aerobic exercise.
  • Avoid Tobacco & Excessive Alcohol.

Patient Counseling (Pharmacist Role)

  • Adherence Importance: Educate that medications must be taken daily, even when asymptomatic.
  • Timing Guidance: Take diuretics in the morning to prevent nocturnal urination (nycturia).
  • OTC Caution: Warn against self-medication with NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Diclofenac) or decongestants as they elevate BP.
  • Home Monitoring: Instruct on proper technique for self-monitoring blood pressure logs.
